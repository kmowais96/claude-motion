# BEFORE YOU OPEN THE QUR’AN

*A visual storytelling film about understanding the Qur’an*

**Estimated duration:** 10–12 minutes (storyboard target: about 12:15)  
**Format:** Narrative documentary · Illustrated 2D animation

The script is designed for a single, warm narrator speaking directly to the viewer. It uses visual metaphors, recurring illustrations, purposeful silence, and a gradual emotional build rather than a conventional lecture.

The film follows one recurring figure: **a reader encountering the Qur’an and gradually discovering how to approach it.** The figure represents the viewer. The animations support the message without attempting to depict Allah, the prophets, or events of revelation directly.

## Visual direction

* **Opening:** Warm, intimate reading space; a quiet wooden desk, a Qur’an, and soft sunlight.
* **Historical context:** Illustrated cities, landscapes, seasons, and everyday life without depictions of the prophets.
* **Understanding:** Connections between scenes shown through flowing timelines, fragments, and visual metaphors.
* **Reflection:** Human-scale scenes of everyday life, choices, and contemplation.

## Production script

### SCENE 01 — THE INVITATION (00:00–00:55)

**Visual:** A quiet room. Morning sunlight slowly reaches a closed Qur’an resting on a desk. A person enters, sits down, and reaches toward the book. Just before opening it, the animation freezes. The ambient sound falls away.

**Voice-over — gentle, curious, unhurried:**

There is something I want you to know...

Before you open the Qur’an.

Something that might change the way you read it.

Because when most of us pick up a book, we already have certain expectations.

We expect it to begin somewhere...

Take us through a series of ideas...

And eventually bring us to a conclusion.

But the Qur’an...

Is different.

Not only because of what it says.

But because of how it speaks.

And to understand that, we need to begin with a question.

What exactly are you about to read?

**Transition:** The page begins turning. The camera follows the page's shadow as it becomes a moving timeline.

### SCENE 02 — A BOOK UNLIKE OTHERS (00:55–02:05)

**Visual:** A conventional book appears as a neat line of consecutive chapters. Its structure transforms into an interconnected sequence of environments: a desert, a city, a gathering place, a night sky, a peaceful home, and a storm at sea. Each forms part of one larger composition.

**Voice-over:**

Imagine opening a book...

And finding that it doesn't follow the pattern you're used to.

One moment, you're reading about the creation of the heavens and the earth.

Then, the story of a prophet.

Then, a warning about the Day of Judgment.

And suddenly, a question.

A question that seems to be speaking directly to you.

You might stop and wonder...

Why is the subject changing?

How are these passages connected?

And how am I supposed to understand them?

Well...

To answer that, you first need to understand something about the Qur’an itself.

**The Qur’an was not revealed all at once as a conventional book.**

It was revealed gradually, over approximately twenty-three years.

And during those years...

Life was happening.

**Transition:** The illustrated timeline expands into a populated historical cityscape. Ordinary people are shown from a distance walking through markets, entering homes, and gathering in public spaces.

### SCENE 03 — REVELATION IN THE MIDST OF LIFE (02:05–03:20)

**Visual:** An illustrated historical environment. Families, travelers, marketplaces, changes in weather, and the passage of seasons. A timeline marks the years of the Prophet's mission without depicting him. The world evolves around a continuous visual thread.

**Voice-over:**

People were asking questions.

They were arguing.

Some were searching for the truth.

Others were resisting it.

There was fear.

There was hope.

There were moments of hardship...

And moments of victory.

And through all of this, Allah revealed guidance to His Messenger, Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him.

Sometimes, the Qur’an answered a question.

Sometimes, it corrected a belief.

Sometimes, it gave comfort to those who were suffering.

And sometimes...

It confronted people with truths they did not want to hear.

The Qur’an addressed real situations, real struggles, and real human beings.

But its guidance was not limited to those who first heard it.

Its message would continue to reach people...

Across generations.

Across languages.

Across the world.

Until today.

Until you.

**Transition:** The historical timeline travels across an illustrated map, through changing centuries and architectural styles, finally arriving back at the reader's desk. The reader's hand rests on the same open page.

### SCENE 04 — THE MISSING CONVERSATION (03:20–04:35)

**Visual:** A modern hallway. A character walks toward a room where two indistinct figures are having a conversation. Their dialogue appears as animated speech shapes rather than readable text. The viewer enters halfway through.

**Voice-over:**

Let me give you an example.

Imagine walking into a room...

In the middle of a conversation.

And the first thing you hear is someone saying...

"How could you do that?"

You understand the words.

Every single one.

But...

Do you understand the situation?

What happened?

Who is being addressed?

Why are they saying it?

Without knowing what came before...

You might misunderstand the entire conversation.

Now, something similar can happen when reading the Qur’an.

You may understand the words of a verse...

But not immediately understand why it was said.

Or who was originally being addressed.

Or what question it was answering.

This is why **context matters**.

And why learning about the life of the Prophet, the circumstances of revelation, and the background of a surah can bring remarkable clarity.

Not because the Qur’an's guidance is trapped in history...

But because knowing the history can help us understand that guidance more accurately.

**Transition:** The conversation freezes. The camera pulls back to reveal the preceding events as illustrated fragments, which assemble into a coherent picture.

### SCENE 05 — WORDS AND MEANING (04:35–05:50)

**Visual:** An animated Arabic word appears over textured paper. A single English translation appears beneath it. As different surrounding phrases emerge, distinct shades of meaning are revealed through visual metaphors such as covering, rejecting, and ingratitude.

**Voice-over:**

But context is only part of the picture.

There's another challenge.

Language.

You see...

Understanding the words of a sentence is not always the same as understanding what the sentence means.

Think about it.

The same words can communicate reassurance...

Or disappointment.

Or a warning.

Depending on how they are spoken...

And the situation in which they are used.

The Qur’an was revealed in Arabic.

And like every rich language, Arabic contains words whose meanings depend on context.

Take the word *kufr*, for example.

You may often see it translated as "disbelief."

But depending on the passage, it can involve rejection, denial, or ingratitude.

These distinctions matter.

And this is one reason why reading a translation alone may not always be enough.

Translations are enormously helpful.

But no translation can reproduce every layer of the original Arabic.

Its precise expressions.

Its rhetorical beauty.

Its subtle connections.

So read translations.

Benefit from them.

But when something seems unclear...

Don't assume you've already understood everything there is to understand.

Go deeper.

**Transition:** A translated phrase remains in the foreground while Arabic text stays behind it. Faint layers of context become visible, then settle into a single illuminated page.

### SCENE 06 — WHO IS BEING ADDRESSED? (05:50–07:00)

**Visual:** The reader examines an illustrated landscape. Different narrative scenes emerge from the page: a traveler navigating a wilderness, a community making a difficult decision, a person offering help, and someone turning away.

**Voice-over:**

Now, there's another question I want you to carry with you.

Whenever you read the Qur’an...

Ask yourself:

Who is being spoken to?

Is it a message to the believers?

A warning to those rejecting the truth?

An instruction to the Prophet?

Or an invitation to humanity?

And when you read about people who lived long before you...

Don't read their stories only to discover what happened.

Ask something more important.

Why is Allah telling me this?

What does this teach me about pride?

About gratitude?

About patience?

About the choices people make?

And what does it teach me about Allah Himself?

Because the Qur’an is not telling these stories merely to preserve history.

It is teaching you through history.

**Transition:** Historical scenes become suspended illustrations. Their shared themes—patience, gratitude, arrogance, sincerity—appear as short typographic labels before returning to the reader.

### SCENE 07 — INFORMATION OR TRANSFORMATION? (07:00–08:20)

**Visual:** Two contrasting paths. On one, a reader rapidly turns page after page as a counter increases. On the other, the reader stops at a passage and reflects. We later see ordinary scenes of making a difficult but truthful choice, helping someone, and admitting a mistake.

**Voice-over:**

And this brings us to something I consider especially important.

The Qur’an is not simply a book you are meant to finish.

It is guidance you are meant to understand.

And live by.

You could read hundreds of pages...

And barely stop to think about what you've read.

Or you could reflect on a single passage...

And discover something that changes your life.

So instead of asking only...

"How much did I read today?"

Ask yourself...

"What did I understand?"

"What is Allah teaching me?"

"What does this tell me about my Creator?"

"What does this reveal about myself?"

And most importantly...

"What am I going to do with what I've learned?"

Because the purpose of understanding guidance...

Is not simply to know more.

It is to become better through what you know.

**Transition:** The page counter fades. Small moments of daily life are connected by a subtle line, showing how understanding can turn into action.

### SCENE 08 — WHEN YOU DON'T UNDERSTAND (08:20–09:20)

**Visual:** The reader reaches a difficult page. The scene becomes darker and filled with overlapping question marks and disconnected fragments. The composition gradually settles as the reader compares passages and opens a commentary.

**Voice-over:**

But I should tell you something.

Not everything will make sense immediately.

You'll come across passages that challenge you.

Teachings that seem unfamiliar.

Names you've never heard.

Events you don't recognize.

And questions you cannot answer.

That's okay.

The problem isn't having questions.

The problem is deciding you already know the answer...

Before you've taken the time to understand.

So be patient.

Look at the verses around the passage.

Learn its background.

Explore reliable explanations.

And ask knowledgeable people when you need help.

Remember...

Understanding the Qur’an is a journey of learning.

You don't have to understand everything on the very first reading.

**Transition:** Scattered question marks rearrange into a path leading through an illustrated landscape. The journey continues beyond the screen.

### SCENE 09 — HOW TO BEGIN (09:20–10:20)

**Visual:** Return to the reading desk. The process is orderly. A surah introduction, Arabic verses, a translation, and explanatory notes appear as separate layers.

**Voice-over:**

So...

How should you begin?

Here's a simple approach.

Before reading a surah, learn a little about it.

Find out what its main themes are.

And what was happening around the time of its revelation.

Then read.

Try to follow the message from beginning to end.

Notice the questions being asked.

The arguments being made.

And the ideas that appear again and again.

When something is difficult, make a note of it.

Return to it later.

Read its explanation.

Take your time.

You are not trying to win a race.

You are trying to understand the guidance of your Creator.

And that deserves your attention.

**Transition:** Five illustrated actions flow together: **Discover → Read → Reflect → Explore → Apply**. Keep typography minimal and legible.

### SCENE 10 — THE QUR’AN AND YOU (10:20–11:40)

**Visual:** A montage of human experiences across different places and times: someone anxious at night, a person helping a neighbor, a family embracing, a traveler beneath the stars, and an individual sitting alone in contemplation. Return periodically to the reader.

**Voice-over:**

And finally...

There's one last thing I want you to remember.

When you open the Qur’an...

Don't read it only as a message that was delivered to people fourteen centuries ago.

Because the world has changed.

Cities have changed.

Languages have changed.

The way we live has changed.

But human beings?

We still struggle with pride.

We still fear losing what we love.

We still chase wealth, recognition, and success.

We still make mistakes.

We still feel regret.

We still search for happiness.

And somewhere along the way...

We still find ourselves asking the same questions.

Why am I here?

What is the purpose of my life?

What is right?

What happens after I die?

And...

What does my Creator expect of me?

The Qur’an invites you to reflect on these questions.

To look at the world around you.

To examine the lives of those who came before you.

And to look within yourself.

Not merely to inform your mind...

But to guide your heart.

And your actions.

**Transition:** The environments fade into a wide, star-filled sky, which dissolves into light falling across the reader's open Qur’an.

### SCENE 11 — THE FIRST PAGE (11:40–12:15)

**Visual:** The reader is seated at the desk again, matching the opening composition. This time the page turns naturally. No elaborate effects—only daylight, stillness, and the beginning of reading.

**Voice-over:**

So come to the Qur’an with sincerity.

Come with questions.

Come willing to learn.

And above all...

Come prepared to listen.

Because the greatest benefit of reading the Qur’an...

Isn't simply being able to say...

"I've finished it."

It's reaching a point where you can say...

"I am beginning to understand."

And by Allah's permission...

That understanding is changing the way I live.

**End:** Pause for three seconds. Hold the final image on the open Qur’an.

**Final title card:**

> Before You Open the Qur’an  
> Read. Reflect. Understand. Live.

## Production notes

### Narrator

One narrator, approximately 115–130 spoken words per minute. Speak like a trusted friend explaining something meaningful, not a documentary announcer performing dramatically. Let important questions breathe.

### Illustration and animation

Use restrained 2D animation, layered parallax, moving camera compositions, and expressive transitions. Avoid changing shots for every sentence; let a single illustration evolve as the narration develops.

### Sound design

Use subtle ambient sounds—turning pages, wind, footsteps, distant everyday activity, and occasional silence. A music-free approach is viable. Avoid making the narration compete with the soundtrack.

### Religious and textual accuracy

Do not illustrate Allah or any prophet. Use environmental imagery for historical scenes. If Arabic Qur’anic text appears, source it from a verified mushaf and proofread it manually rather than generating it as decorative image text. Distinguish the narrator's reflections from direct Qur’anic quotations.

### Timing and editorial notes

The timestamps are storyboard targets, not frame-accurate durations. Time scenes to the recorded voice performance. The narrative arc moves from curiosity and confusion toward discovery, understanding, and an invitation to begin.

*This is an original script inspired by general guidance drawn from the introductory ideas of Tafhīm al-Qur’ān. It is not a verbatim quotation or translation of Maududi. The theme should be humble. No face visible.*
