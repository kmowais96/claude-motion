# Paper-cut style — read from the reference

Source: the one frame I could actually see (the video's thumbnail, cropped to its
motion-graphics inset). **I could not see the video itself** — YouTube redirects
this machine to a captcha, and its three auto-extracted frames are all talking-head
shots. So this describes the *look* with confidence and the *motion* only by
inference. Correct me on the motion.

## What the reference is doing

A physical diorama, shot with a real camera: everything is cut paper and card,
stacked, lit from one side, with genuine depth of field.

**Layering is the whole trick.** No element is one flat shape. Each is a stack:
a cream top layer, then an orange-red layer offset 4–8 px down-right, then its
cast shadow. That offset is what reads as thickness. The social icons (heart,
speech bubble, share arrow) are the clearest example — same silhouette, three
layers, visibly extruded.

**Edges are torn, not cut.** The window in the dark slab has a ragged fibrous
edge with the pale paper core showing through. Clean geometric edges appear only
where something is meant to read as manufactured.

**The palette is three values and one accent:**

| Hex | Role |
|---|---|
| `#141414` | charcoal card — the slab, the ground |
| `#E8DFCE` | cream/manila paper — every top layer |
| `#C9C2B4` | paper in shadow |
| `#D94F2B` | orange-red — under-layers, highlights, threads. The only colour. |

Photographs are **always greyscale**. Colour belongs to the paper, never the image.

**Texture:** paper fibre visible on every torn edge; halftone dot screen on the
icon faces; film grain over everything.

**Depth:** red threads hang vertically through the frame, crossing in *front* of
the subject. The background is out-of-focus newsprint pinned to a wall. The
subject plane is tilted — the camera looks down and across it, never flat-on.
Strong vignette.

## Motion (inferred, needs checking)

Stop-motion collage: stepped easing, 2–3 frame holds, no smooth interpolation.
Elements arrive by sliding in with a paper-drag, dropping with a stamp settle, or
popping on in single-frame steps. The camera makes one slow committed move per
shot. Nothing morphs — paper does not morph.

## What this changes for the du'a reel

The amber line-art `StoryReel` and this are different films. Carried over: the
door as the through-line, the narration, the card timing. Replaced: the glowing
vector motif becomes a built object in paper; the black field becomes charcoal
card; amber becomes the orange-red accent.

The doors are the gift here — a door cut from layered card, opening by a real
hinge, is exactly what this technique is best at.
